Live + Review

Simulated camera playback and systematic recorded-video inspection, connected to the same evidence and budget ledger.

1.1 The product in one sentence

Safety and operations staff can inspect a recording, review timestamped concerns and cited guidance, record a decision, and export a self-contained incident report under a fixed model-spending limit.

Evidence status: offline engineering checks pass, and a hosted Live batch on six real videos has been measured: 47 ten-second windows inspected, 3 videos fully covered, $0.418 of model usage. Independently labeled accuracy, a provider invoice and the real-user pilot remain open; this document says so rather than substituting projections.

This is a human review aid, not an injury predictor, regulatory certification or autonomous safety controller. Local storage and orchestration do not imply that hosted inference stays on the device.

Motivation & intended user

2. Make review gaps visible

A safety or operations reviewer needs to turn footage into a defensible next step. A short AI answer can be persuasive while hiding uninspected intervals, ambiguous pixels or an unfinished specialist review. This project makes those omissions visible.

2.1 One bounded task

Review a recording up to ten minutes long for acute events (struck-by, falls, fire/burn) and four routine visible-condition categories: PPE visibility, blocked access, unstable stacks, and slip/trip hazards. Preserve the exact evidence, state uncertainty and let a person decide what to investigate or correct.

LiveReview
Select a file and play it as a camera at 1×. Pause stops source-time progress.Upload or choose a library recording. Inspect every consecutive 10-second window, including the final tail.

2.2 What the reviewer receives

A coverage timeline, concern list, exact evidence stills, specialist/challenge results, source excerpts, a budget state and an append-only human decision. The exported HTML contains its images and remains readable after the app is closed.

2.3 Benefit to test

The hypothesis is shorter review time and fewer unsupported conclusions while retaining useful findings. Those benefits require a manual-versus-assisted pilot. They are not established by the working interface or fixture tests.

The available contact is a safety/operations user. Their task results, consented feedback and corrections have not yet been collected.

Workflow & interface

4. One evidence trail, two modes

4.2 Consecutive coverage

A ten-minute review plans 60 windows. Each keeps up to two motion-selected sequences of six ordered frames (lead-in, peak and aftermath), sent as packets of at most six images. Each validated result is saved immediately. A failed decode, missing second distinct sample or uninspected interval prevents an “all windows inspected” claim. Complete coverage means the declared samples were inspected, not that every decoded frame or hazard was seen.

4.3 Durability and human review

The source hash, original configuration, ledger and immutable observations survive restart. Known completed windows are skipped on resume; uncertain charges block automatic replay. Every concern remains in the report, even when the short answer would exceed six claims. Human decisions are server-identified and append-only.

Adjacent observations merge only with matching source, camera, category, PPE item where relevant, and localized box IoU ≥0.5. This is conservative spatial continuity, not identity recognition. Missing localization keeps concerns separate.

5.2 What the cap trades off

The cap binds on busy footage: one 197-second video raised 37 concerns, but only 7 received specialist review before its $0.15 limit. Sampled frames reduce image cost but can miss short events. A crop can improve a visible detail but cannot recover an unseen moment. Independent challenge can reject an unsupported conclusion but adds cost and delay. Prioritizing complete interval screening leaves less allowance for specialists. The UI must expose that unfinished work rather than imply full assessment.

5.3 Experiment feasibility

Twenty-four clips times two workflows means 48 runs: about $4 at the measured mean and $7.20 at the cap, across several daily budget windows. Do not raise the approved caps or promise the full experiment by the deadline without measured costs.

5.4 Comparison to perform

Safety & demonstrated defense

6. Unsupported evidence is rejected

The system recommends actions for human review. It has no equipment-control or external-message-delivery tool. A model judgment is not a human label or a compliance finding merely because it sounds confident.

RiskImplemented defenseRemaining limitation
Hallucinated or foreign evidenceExact retained-frame/media/time checks; source hash verification; cited exact clauses and applicability gates.Pixels and clauses can still be misinterpreted.
Malicious visual text / source contentTreat retrieved text and visible text as evidence; scoped tools and independent challenge.No paid adversarial vision evaluation yet.
Future frames or stale camera resultsSealed source-time windows, generation checks, no forward seek in Live.Slow processing can drop pending windows; they remain gaps.
Loops and overspendingBounded agent requests/tools/tokens/deadlines, shared atomic reservations, no automatic retry with uncertain billing.Provider billing needs reconciliation; caps can prevent completion.
Corrupted network uploadsOnly provably unsent requests settle at $0 and resend once; ambiguous failures keep an unknown charge and stop paid work. Hosted batch: 3 recovered, 1 stop in 362 requests.The network fault itself is outside the app.
Unsafe output or urgencyEscaped standalone report; provisional priority separate from supported urgency; human disposition.Human review quality and site context still matter.

6.1 Demonstrated offline defense

A test injects a Live screening job for a window ending at 10,000 ms while the source-time cutoff is 1,000 ms. The coordinator rejects the future scope before any model request or saved chunk. Related tests reject foreign/invented/wrong-time citations and modified handoffs. A report test submits a script tag in a human reason and verifies escaped inert output.

Observed result: these deterministic checks pass in the local test suite. They demonstrate boundary enforcement, not immunity to all prompt injection or visual hallucination.

Evidence: backend/tests/test_review_runs.py, test_video_citation_boundary.py, test_video_safety_layers.py, and the verification receipt.

7.4 Metric definitions

Precision is matched predicted events divided by scored predictions. Recall is matched events divided by certain human events. Match only the same clip/category and use one prediction per label. Ambiguous labels and matching predictions are reported separately. Full-workflow predictions require a reviewed substantive finding without challenge issues; pending or insufficient cases remain abstentions and can reduce recall.

Report completed versus planned windows independently of precision. Short clips cannot establish false alarms per camera-hour. Do not tune on the held-out set.

Dataset: DOI 10.17632/xjmtb22pff.1, CC BY 4.0. Temporal evaluation and failure inspection draw on FiftyOne documentation and causal evaluation on StreamingBench. No benchmark score is claimed.

8.2 Current limits

Sampling can miss fast events. Spatial boxes do not establish person identity. Site context, event date and jurisdiction default to unknown. A specialist sees a bounded evidence packet; omitted or later occurrences remain visible and need review. Live analysis can trail playback. This network corrupts a few percent of large uploads; an unprovable failure stops paid work by design. A paused local browser is not a production surveillance service. The single local operator session is not enterprise authentication.
